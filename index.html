Rules that apply every phase
  • Sip, don't gulp — max ~150 ml at a time, small sips over minutes, no straws
  • Chew until completely mashed before swallowing (aim for ~25 chews) once solids start
  • Leave at least 30 minutes between eating solids and drinking
  • Stop the moment you feel satisfied — don't push to "finish the portion"
  • Every waking hour: a 5-minute walk and 5 minutes of deep breathing, to protect against blood clots and chest complications
  • 1.5–2 L fluids a day, spaced through the day, not with meals
  • No added sugar, caffeine, carbonation, alcohol, or citrus until your surgeon clears it
  • Small plates and spoons; light walking daily, easing into 20–30 min of light exercise from week 2

How to drink this early on

Sip 30–150 ml at a time, roughly every 15–30 minutes rather than a large glass at once — big swallows stretch the new stomach and can bring on nausea or a dumping-syndrome-like reaction. Room-temperature liquids usually sit better than iced ones in week 1.

If you're struggling to get enough in, Alexolyte (rehydration solution) sipped like juice through the day, half a cup morning and half at night, helps prevent the dizziness and low blood pressure that come with under-hydration.

Warning signs of not drinking enough: dizziness, dry mouth, bad breath, headache, nausea, constipation, and dark urine. See the Guide tab if these show up.

Watch for
Dehydration
Signs: dark urine, dry mouth, dizziness, headache, dry/sagging skin.
Helps: steady small sips between meals rather than large amounts at once; less tea/coffee (they're diuretics).
Dumping syndrome
Usually settles within 12–18 months. Triggered by sugar (even honey or juice), drinking soon after eating, fatty food, or alcohol.
Within 30 min: cramping, bloating, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, headache, fatigue, low blood pressure.
1–3 hours later: sweating, shakiness, confusion, sudden hunger.
If it happens: 1 tbsp honey/jam or ½ cup juice, then 15–30 min later a small protein + veg bite (e.g. cottage cheese with cucumber).
Vomiting
Usually from eating too fast, not chewing enough, or too much volume at once. Persistent trouble with one food (often chicken, red meat, rice, fatty food) may just mean set it aside and retry later.
Reflux / heartburn
Drink between meals, not with them. Don't lie down within 2 hours of eating; raise the head of the bed. Skip salty, fried, spicy, carbonated, or citrus foods and caffeine for now.
Constipation
Common early on from less movement and lower fluids/fibre. Helps: light walking, the full 1.5–2 L of fluids, fresh vegetables once they're allowed.
Hair thinning
Common after bariatric surgery from the nutrition shift — staying consistent with the vitamins and whey protein is the main defence. Worth a dermatology visit at the clinic if it's heavy.
Contact the surgical team if
  • Vomiting won't stop, or you can't keep fluids down at all
  • Fever, or spreading redness/pain at the wound sites
  • Severe or worsening abdominal pain
  • Very dark urine or no urination
  • Dehydration signs that don't improve after resting and sipping fluids
